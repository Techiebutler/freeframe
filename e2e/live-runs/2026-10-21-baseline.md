what the app does today, so later phases have a before and after.

- Commit under test: `069abdcd` on `feat/upload-queue-1-server`. `apps/` and `packages/` are byte-identical to

## Deviations from the brief and the protocol

1. **No custom 2 MB/s.** The DevTools MCP can only apply presets. I used **Fast 4G**, the fastest: 168 750 B/s up,
   1 012 500 B/s down, 165 ms latency (the MCP's own preset table). The panel's per-file speeds were 22 to 56
   KB/s; at 168 750 B/s the whole 291.6 MiB would take about 30 minutes, so it was never meant to finish
   throttled.
2. **I lifted the throttle instead of cancelling.** At **+167.7 s** I cleared the emulation (the protocol's Q1 rule
   is "Fast 4G for 160 s, sample, then lift"; this lift followed the +159 s sample by 8 s). All 62 part PUTs and
   all 50 `complete` calls then finished within 3 seconds, so the run reached a real end state for every row
   and nothing was cancelled (no `/upload/abort` was sent).
3. The panel was on its default **Active** tab for the row samples, so they count unfinished rows only.
4. Row samples start at the first 500 ms tick (t = 502 ms), not at the instant of the drop.

What the numbers say about today's behaviour:

- **All 50 initiates, then all 62 presigns, are handed to `fetch` within 1.7 s of the drop**, and the PUTs follow
  within 4 s. There is no bound on concurrency anywhere in the app; the only limit is Chrome's six connections per host.
- **Head-of-line blocking by the first parts to arrive.** While throttled, the six storage sockets stayed on the
  first PUTs to be sent (the first six pending PUTs in the DevTools list belong to four uploads, two of which have
  two parts in flight, so they are multipart files), and the other 55 PUTs, small files included, waited in the
  browser's queue: after 159 s only 2 of 62 PUTs had finished and 49 rows were still "Uploading" (screenshot 2,
  the visible rows: the 40, 28, 25 and 22 MiB files at 0%, the 19 and 16 MiB files at 28% and 51%). Starting
  everything at once did not make the small files finish sooner.
- **Everything finished correctly once bandwidth came back**: no retries, no failed rows, no error.
- **A refetch burst after completions.** The project grid refetched `GET /projects/<id>/assets` and
  `GET /projects/<id>/folders` in pairs after completions: more than 50 pairs in the burst after the throttle
  was lifted (reqids 524 to 660 alone), and further pairs from polling afterwards. Not part of Q1, noted for the
  phase that touches `use-upload-refetch`.

## Q2 toolchain smoke (not a Q2 result)

To check the trace tooling, not to judge Q2: 40 files of 200 KB (not 300), dropped on a fresh project
("Live Q2 trace smoke 2026-10-21", `80d21c94-392e-4e1c-a44c-78dcad972baa`) with a performance trace running and no
throttling, with a second, idle tab of the same account open.

- All 40 uploads completed (40 initiate, 40 presign, 40 part PUT, 40 complete, all 200); the app had up to 40
  initiates, 37 presigns, 24 part PUTs and 34 completes open at once.
- Main-thread long tasks in the 26.1 s trace window, from the saved trace (`RunTask` on the renderer that ran the
  page, the one with the most tasks: 9 307 tasks, 3.3 s busy): 1 task over 50 ms, **1 over 200 ms, worst 263 ms**. The probe's PerformanceObserver saw
  the same 262 ms task. The idle second tab's renderer also showed one 259 ms task during the run; cause not investigated
  (the two tabs share `ff-uploads` through localStorage, which may be it).
- Indicative only: tracing adds overhead, the file count is 13% of Q2's, and this was one run. It does suggest
  that Q2's "no task over 200 ms" is not met on `main` even at 40 files; the real number needs the 300-file run.
