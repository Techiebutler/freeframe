## Deviations from the brief and the protocol

1. **The chrome-devtools MCP could not start its browser**: its profile was held by another session's Chrome (pid 53237, as in
   phases 4 and 5), which was left alone. Section 10's fallback instead: two headed Google Chromes started by this pass, each with
   its own profile and DevTools port, driven by `live-tools/cdp-driver.js` (Playwright 1.59 `connectOverCDP`). As in phases 4 and
   5, every page read `visible` and focused (Playwright's focus emulation).
2. **The MCP's tools, through the driver** (new driver commands in this pass, `LIVE-CHROME.md` section 10):
   - **performance trace**: `trace` (Playwright's Chromium `browser.startTracing`, the protocol's `Tracing` domain, devtools.timeline
     categories), analysed with section 5 item 8's script; plus an in-page `requestAnimationFrame` sampler for frame times and the
     probe's `longtask` PerformanceObserver. Not `performance_analyze_insight`.
   - **memory**: `metrics` (the protocol's `Performance.getMetrics` and `Runtime.getHeapUsage`, with `HeapProfiler.collectGarbage`
     for the start, middle and end samples) next to `performance.memory` read in the page.
   - **GIFs**: `screencast` (`Page.startScreencast` JPEG frames) assembled with ffmpeg by `live-tools/frames-to-gif.js` (new), not
     `gif_creator`. `screencapture` has no Screen Recording permission here.
   - **file inputs**: `filesraw` (`DOM.setFileInputFiles` with the paths, as the MCP's `upload_file` does). The driver's old `files`
     (Playwright's `setInputFiles`) refuses more than 50 MB to a `connectOverCDP` browser and hands the page **in-memory copies**,
     so a `touch` or `rm` on disk would not reach the page (it would have made U1 and U2 vacuous). Every file in this pass went in
     with `filesraw`; the GIF frames and the U1/U2 results show the browser reading the real files.
   - `press` (a key on the keyboard) and the `Fast 3G` / `Slow 3G` presets (the MCP's table) were added too.
3. **Clicks**: the account menu, Log out, the confirmation's buttons, Notifications, the sidebar's Projects link, the Uploads
   button, the version switcher and the invite form's submit were trusted clicks (the driver's `click`). The panel's own row
   buttons (Rename, Cancel upload, Resume, Cancel all queued) and the "Open in viewer" link in take 2 of V1 were clicked from the
   page (`element.click()`, untrusted), as in the in-page stand-ins of phases 2 and 3.
4. **Stand-ins** (`LIVE-CHROME.md` section 9): `hold()` / `release()` (Q3, Q4, C1 to C3, U1 to U3, N2, S3, S4, R1, R3, V1, P2, A1 to
   A3, L1 to L3); `block(/:19000/)` for S2 (part PUTs fail as network errors, not 503); `pace(5 MiB/s)` for P1; `delayPartsMs` for
   R3; the stand-in file chooser (`pickNext`) for the Resumes and New Version. S1, S3 and S4 used the real faults (`docker stop`,
   `docker network disconnect` of `ffe2e-minio-1` only, both restored, health checked, aliases `minio` and `ffe2e-minio-1` back).
5. **The long run ran beside the scenarios**: Chrome B's 500 files at Fast 3G (08:59:48 to 09:29:58 UTC) overlapped Q1 to L2 in
   Chrome A (another browser, another account; the throttle is per page). The global faults (S1, S3, S4, P1's cap) waited until both
   the long run and the video were done.
